Methodology + sources

The exposure score is a weighted average of 4 sliders (third-party hosting 30%, customer-facing 25%, regulated industry 25%, US/EU jurisdiction 20%), with each contributing 0-25 points to a 0-100 scale. The matrix classifies each cell as Triggered (active reporting obligation), Advisory (regime covers in principle, no crystallised obligation), or None (no surface).

  • California AG vs OpenAI (Nov 17): arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/california-sues-openai-to-stop-public-nuisance-of-out-of-control-ai-agents — 80 incidents, 27 reported, public nuisance + unfair competition laws.
  • APRA AI stress tests (Nov 13): abc.net.au/news/2026-11-13/regulator-apra-warns-of-interruptions-as-frontier-ai-evolves/107378944 — Lonsdale statement, mandatory safeguards, first tests 2027.
  • US banks disclaiming agent liability (Nov 18): techcrunch.com/2026/11/18/more-banks-says-customers-using-ai-agents-are-on-their-own-even-if-they-get-hacked — Citi, JPMorgan, BofA, PNC ToS updates.
  • Fireworks AI breach (Nov 12): techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/cyberattack-on-fireworks-ai-allowed-hackers-to-access-notion-customers-chat-transcripts — LLM-API supply-chain attack, Notion chat transcripts stolen.
  • EU AI Act incident-reporting requirements: Articles 73 + 74 — providers of high-risk AI must report serious incidents to market surveillance authorities within 15 days. The Act has been in force since Aug 2024 with phased applicability.
  • ASIC design-and-distribution obligations (Corp Act Pt 7.7A): applies to AI-driven financial product recommendations, including agentic financial advice.

This is a diagnostic tool, not legal advice. It maps exposure, not remediation. For deployment decisions, consult qualified legal counsel in your jurisdiction.
